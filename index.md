# Data Description 
The purpose of the study is to observe the effect of Pacific dulse (Devaleraea mollis) seaweed consumption on the cardiometabolic health and gut microbiome of overweight and obese adults. A double-blind, randomized, parallel study will be conducted in 30 overweight or obese adults aged 25 to 65. Subjects will be randomly assigned to consume a roll of bread (50 g) per day, either containing blanched freeze-dried Pacific dulse powder (two grams/day) or without, over a four-week duration. 
# Roles and Responsibilities 
This research project is an additional component of the broader USDA-funded project in Pacific dulse seaweed. The implementation of the data management plan is primarily the PI’s responsibility. They will serve as the project’s data manager and be responsible for archiving and preservation, protection of sensitive and protected data, and instrumentation maintenance. The graduate student will be responsible for collecting data, such as the Qualtrics survey, screening questionnaires, and biological data, as well as analyzing the data. Additionally, data organization, metadata generation, quality control, and access control will be conducted by the graduate student. The study nurse who is responsible for collecting blood samples from subjects will assist with data documentation and metadata generation. Stool samples will be sent to a third company for analysis. Upon receiving the data, the graduate student will be responsible for storing it securely with all other data. Additional lab members may assist in data collection, specifically when using an Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) to measure the blood levels of human hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), fasting insulin, insulin sensitivity, total cholesterol, LDL- HDL-cholesterol, triglycerides, CRP, IL-6, and TNF-α. However, they will not be responsible for managing the data; the graduate student will be recording all results while the other lab members assist with conducting the actual analysis. Ultimately, there are three people with data management responsibilities: the PI, the study nurse, and the graduate student. If any of them leave the project, they would have to make sure that they document all data sources before leaving. Once they leave the project, all access to the data will be removed to ensure no data will be shared outside of the research lab.
# Data Standards and Metadata 
As this project is USDA-funded, a data management plan was required before initiating any process. The PI was responsible for compiling a plan to identify how the data would be collected, stored, secured, and shared with other researchers. This has been approved by the USDA. In addition to compiling a data management plan, this project requires approval by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) because it involves human research subjects. In the application, there are multiple questions regarding who has access to the data and how it will be managed. It was explicitly stated that only the PI, the study nurse, and the graduate student will have access to the data and will be stored securely.
# Storage and Security 
All research records, including the subjects’ personal information, will be stored on an encrypted cloud platform (OneDrive) secured by the university server, with access restricted to the PI, the study nurse, and the graduate student. All hard copy documents, such as the signed consent forms, will be stored securely in a locked cabinet with access restricted to the PI and the graduate student.  
# Access and Data Sharing 
Access to all data is restricted to the PI, the study nurse, and the graduate student. Data will not be shared with any researchers outside the lab. 
# Archiving and Preservation 
All data gathered online, such as the cardiometabolic health measures of subjects, will be regularly backed up to OneDrive managed by the PI. This process is manual; it is the graduate student’s responsibility to ensure that all data is regularly backed up. The PI is responsible for ensuring that all data is backed up securely and that no other researchers have access to the data. A backup of these data will be stored on a USB, which will be stored in the same locked cabinet as where the hard copy documents will be stored. Since the data is stored in a cloud system, all data will be stored in a shared file that the PI will also have access to. This ensures that no data will be lost after the graduate student’s graduation.
